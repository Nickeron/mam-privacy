1. Scope and Audience

This policy describes how the app collects, uses, and stores information when you play Mommy a Monster! on Android.

The app's social features (friends, Quick-Chat, groups, gifts, invite codes and the weekly league) are optional. They open only after you choose to join them, and only for players who confirm they are 13 or older. This policy covers them in section 2.7.

This app is not designed for, marketed to, or directed at children under 13 years of age. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 has provided information through the app, please contact us at the address above and we will delete it promptly.

2. What We Collect and Why

2.1 Google Play Games identity, leaderboard, and achievements

When you sign in with Google Play Games (GPGS), the app receives an anonymous pseudonymous player identifier assigned by Google. This identifier is scoped to your Google Play Games account and is not your name, email address, or any other directly identifying information. The app never reads your Google account name or email.

We use this identifier to submit your score to the in-game leaderboard and to report your progress on Google Play Games achievements. Google acts as a data processor on our behalf for this service. Signing in with Google Play Games is optional — if you decline, you can still play; the leaderboard and achievements features simply are not available. Cloud save does not depend on GPGS sign-in; it is described exclusively in Section 2.2.

2.2 Firebase anonymous identity and Firestore cloud save

On first launch the app creates an anonymous Firebase account, which yields a random pseudonymous user identifier (UID) generated by Google's Firebase Authentication. This UID is not your name, email address, or any other directly identifying information, and is not linked to your Google account. It is stored on your device and persists across launches so the app can recognise the same install. If you link the game to Google Play Games (in Settings, or when you join the social features), we link this UID to your Google Play Games account so you can restore your progress on another device.

When an anonymous Firebase account exists, the app stores a copy of your game-progress data in Google's Cloud Firestore database, in a document keyed to your anonymous Firebase UID. Access is restricted so each document can be read or written only by the anonymous account that owns it. Your local on-device save is always authoritative; the cloud copy is an additive backup used to restore progress and sync across devices. Google acts as a data processor on our behalf.

2.3 Game progress stored locally

The following game state is stored on your device (in Android app storage and a private save file): level progress (stars earned, highest level), currency balances (coins, diamonds, stars, cookie-jar balance), hero and monster unlock states, boost upgrade levels, total playtime (a relative counter used for save-conflict resolution), quest and reward-pass state, achievement progress and unlocks, lifetime gameplay statistics, cosmetic items owned and equipped, and one-time purchase entitlement flags. This data is held on your device and, as described in Section 2.2, backed up to Cloud Firestore keyed to your anonymous Firebase identifier. It is not shared with any third party other than Google as the cloud-save processor.

2.4 In-app purchases

When you make a real-money purchase, payment is processed entirely by Google Play Billing — we never receive or store your payment card details, billing address, or any payment-method information.

To confirm a purchase is genuine before granting the item (fraud prevention), the app sends the Google Play purchase token to a Firebase Cloud Function we operate. That function verifies the token with the Google Play Developer API and stores a minimal validation record in Google's Cloud Firestore: the purchase token, your pseudonymous Firebase user ID, the product identifier, the Google Play order ID, a validation timestamp and, if Google Play reports the purchase refunded or cancelled, when we reversed it. This record exists to stop a purchase from being granted more than once and, if Google Play reports the purchase refunded or cancelled, to take back the currency it granted; it contains no payment or contact information. If you redeem a promotional code, we likewise record that your pseudonymous Firebase user ID claimed that code, to stop the same code being claimed twice. We also keep your currency balances on our server (your wallet), so a purchase is never granted twice and your balances stay right across your devices. Each change to your wallet is recorded, and that record is deleted after 90 days. The purchase also generates an analytics event as described in Section 2.5.

2.5 Analytics (consent-gated)

On first launch, the app shows a consent dialog. Analytics collection begins only if you actively consent. If you decline or do not respond, no analytics events are sent. You can withdraw consent at any time from the in-app Settings menu.

If you consent, we collect the following via Firebase Analytics (a Google product):

  • A pseudonymous per-install identifier assigned by the Firebase SDK on installation. It is not linked to your Google account or any other identity and resets on uninstall/reinstall.
  • Gameplay event parameters — level numbers, star counts, attempt counts, boost types used, purchase SKUs and amounts, tutorial step identifiers, and session counts. No parameter contains a name, email, location, or any personally identifying value.
  • Social action counts: if you use the social features and allow analytics, we count when you first reach the social hub, accept a friend request, send a Quick-Chat phrase or sticker, send a gift, create or join a group, complete your first ranked league run of a week, or use the Share button for your friend code. These records carry no player name, friend code, group name or message content.
  • Device and OS metadata — device model, Android OS version, and country (country-code granularity only). This is collected automatically by the Firebase SDK.

Purpose: to understand how players engage with the game so we can improve it. Processor: Firebase Analytics is operated by Google LLC; data is transmitted to and stored on Google's servers in the United States under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. A copy of the consented analytics events is also exported once per day to Google BigQuery, our analytics warehouse, into a dataset located in the European Union. The exported data is the same pseudonymous event data described above; no additional data is collected by the export. Retention: Google Analytics retains user-level event data for 14 months, after which individual event records are deleted from Google's analytics systems. The copies exported to our BigQuery warehouse are retained for up to 24 months; exported records expire and are deleted automatically at the end of that window.

We do not collect your name, email, phone number, or postal address; precise (GPS) location; advertising identifiers (we use no advertising SDK); device identifiers such as the Android ID (the app reads the Android ID locally to derive a hashed device-binding value that protects your purchase wallet against cloned save data; that value is stored only on your device and is never transmitted to us or to anyone else); camera, microphone, contacts, or calendar data; or any information from your device's files beyond the app's own save data.

2.6 Crash reports (consent-gated)

Crash and stability reporting is provided by Firebase Crashlytics (a Google product). Like analytics, crash collection is consent-gated: it is off by default and is enabled only if you actively consent via the first-launch dialog. You can withdraw consent at any time in Settings.

If you consent, when the app crashes or records a non-fatal error we collect device and OS metadata (device model, manufacturer, Android OS version, app version, and device state such as free memory and orientation), a crash stack trace, and diagnostic keys/logs the app attaches to aid debugging (e.g. the current level number). These contain no name, email, location, or other personally identifying value.

Processor: Firebase Crashlytics is operated by Google LLC; data is stored on Google's servers in the United States under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework. Retention: 90 days (the Firebase default).

You can join the social features after you win the first night. To join, you accept our community rules and confirm that you are 13 or older. Other players never see your user ID or your Google account. They see your public name, which you pick from our list of word pairs, your avatar look, and what you choose to send them. Your friends also see your profile card (2.7.4). Players in your league group see your public name, avatar look, pinned badge and weekly score (2.7.8). If the age check in 2.7.2 locks social features for you after you joined, other players stop seeing you. They see you again only after the lock ends and you confirm again that you are 13 or older.

2.8 Age questions

When you first open the app, we ask whether you are 13 or older and, if you are, whether you are 16 or older. Your answers stay on your device. They decide whether we can offer you analytics and crash reporting. We never ask for your date of birth.

If you join the social features, we ask again whether you are 13 or older. That answer is stored on our server; see section 2.7.2.

6. Your Rights Under GDPR

If you are located in the European Economic Area, you have the rights to: access a copy of your personal data; rectification of inaccurate data; erasure (“right to be forgotten”); restriction of processing; objection to processing; withdrawal of consent (for analytics and crash reporting, at any time via in-app Settings — withdrawal does not affect the lawfulness of processing before withdrawal); and data portability.

When you ask for a copy of your data, we do not tell you who reported you (UAVG Article 41).

You can object at any time to the safety records in section 2.7 that we keep on the basis of our legitimate interest. Write to the address below.

For erasure: uninstalling the app removes the local copy of your game progress. To delete the Firestore cloud save, your anonymous Firebase account, your currency wallet, the purchase-validation and promo-redemption records, and your social records (section 2.7), except what section 5 says we keep after you delete your account, contact us at the address below and we will run our account-deletion routine — the steps are described on our account & data deletion page. In the app, open Settings, tap Privacy & Account, then tap Delete Account. For analytics data, we will process a deletion request for your app_instance_id if you can provide it, or by deleting the relevant event stream, and we will delete the rows keyed to that same identifier from our BigQuery warehouse.

You also have the right to lodge a complaint with the Dutch data protection authority, the Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens (AP), at autoriteitpersoonsgegevens.nl.

To exercise any of these rights, contact piratebeer2019@gmail.com. We answer every request within one month of receiving it. If we need to check that a request comes from you, we ask at once.

7. Security

The personal data we hold on servers we operate includes your game-progress cloud save (Section 2.2), your currency wallet, your social records (section 2.7), and the minimal purchase-validation and promo-redemption records described in Section 2.4. It is stored in Google's Cloud Firestore or, once a service has moved, in Cloudflare's D1 database (Section 4). Access rules restrict your cloud save so it can be read or written only by the anonymous Firebase account that owns it. Clients have no direct access to the purchase-validation and promo-redemption records; only server code we control writes them. Game progress is also stored on your device (under Android's private app storage, inaccessible to other apps). The app sends data to Firebase and to our Cloudflare Workers over HTTPS, so it is encrypted in transit; we do not transmit raw personal data over unencrypted connections.
